Energy savings claims need proof methodology, not percentages

By Nilesh Pande, Founder and Lead Engineer, Konark Process Instruments

Why this matters

Every furnace vendor can quote a percentage saving. Most cannot show you how the number was measured. For a plant head defending an investment to a board, the methodology matters more than the number, because the number without a method cannot be verified — and unverifiable claims die in procurement.

The honest way to measure combustion savings

Savings on a furnace come from three places, and each needs its own measurement:

  1. Fuel per tonne of product — measured before and after on the same production mix, normalised for utilisation
  2. Waste-heat recovery — measured as preheated combustion air temperature and the fuel reduction it produces at the same firing rate
  3. Downtime and rejects — false trips and off-spec thermal cycles cost fuel twice: once in the lost batch, once in the reheat

A credible claim states: what was measured, over what period, at what utilisation, and who validated it.

Red flags in vendor claims

  • A single percentage with no baseline period stated
  • Savings quoted from a different furnace type or fuel
  • No mention of production mix or utilisation normalisation
  • Payback calculated from nameplate values instead of metered fuel flow

What an owner should request

  1. Baseline: metered fuel consumption over a defined period before the retrofit
  2. Post-implementation: the same meter, same period, same utilisation
  3. A site visit protocol: who validates, what data is shared, when
  4. A savings-sharing or guarantee clause only if both sides can see the meter

"We have built self-recuperative burner management for reheating furnaces, and the discipline we insist on is measurement: the savings live in the fuel meter, not in a slide deck. We will walk a plant head through exactly what we measured, how, and over what period — and we only claim what the meter shows."
